What everything does

ScaleTune is for instruments whose intonation you have to make yourself — strings, winds, voice. Everything here is a reference to play against, not a thing to listen to.

Modes

Drone
Holds notes indefinitely, to tune against. Several sound together, so a drone can be a chord.
Scale
Plays a scale as a line, in the key, range and direction you choose.
Arpeggio
The same, built from a chord instead of a scale.
Free
You pick the notes yourself, each with its own octave, and they play in that order.
Metro
The metronome on its own, with no notes at all.
Tuner
Listens instead of sounding. Nothing else plays while it is open — the microphone would hear the app itself.

Tuner

What it reads
The note, how many cents away you are, and both frequencies — the one you are playing and the one you want. Under two cents it turns green.
The strobe
The band of stripes drifts at the difference between the two pitches: still means right, and it drifts the way you are wrong. It shows a quarter of a cent, which no needle can — at A4 that is a tenth of a hertz, one slow crawl every ten seconds. A long way out it blurs, which is what the number above is for.
Auto and Lock
Auto names the nearest note. Lock holds it to one string, so it will not jump to the neighbour when you are half a semitone below — which is exactly when you need it to hold still.
Reference A4
415 to 466, moving every note with it. 415 for baroque pitch, 442 or 443 where the orchestra sits high.
Temperament
Equal, just, Pythagorean, quarter-comma meantone, Werckmeister III. Each is a set of deviations from equal temperament, counted from the key you name, so the key note is the one everything else is made pure against. Just makes the thirds pure and the fifths nearly so; Pythagorean makes the fifths pure and the thirds wide; meantone buys pure thirds by narrowing every fifth; Werckmeister spreads the error so that every key is playable and none is identical.
What Key does to the reading
In just intonation in C, an A sits about fifteen cents under an equal A — and the tuner will say so. That is not an error to correct; it is what the temperament is. Reference A4 keeps its own job, which is to place the equal grid that the deviations are counted from.
Low strings
It reads down to 27.5 Hz, below the lowest string on a five-string bass. Down there it answers more slowly: a pitch that low needs a fifth of a second of sound before there is anything to measure.

Root

The twelve notes
The key everything else is built from.
Single / Study list
Single works on one scale or chord at a time. Study list collects several and takes them in turn, one per pass, so you can work a whole family without stopping.

Scales and chords

The family boxes
One opens at a time and the others fold away. A closed box still says what is chosen inside it, so folding one never hides a selection.
Sevenths in one octave
A seventh arpeggio over one octave stops below its top note, the way inversions are practised. Over more than one octave it lands on it.
Clear
Empties the selection, or the whole study list.

Range & Direction

Octaves
How far the line reaches. If the top would run past the highest sample, the span is reduced until it fits — and grows back by itself once there is room again.
Start Octave
Where the line begins.
Direction
Up, down, or up then back down.
Start On
Which degree of the scale the line starts from — the way a mode is practised from each of its own notes.

Practice Progressions

Root Movement
Stay in one key, or move up a fourth each time everything else has been round once — the cycle of fourths.
Speed Up
Adds so many BPM every pass, up to 300.
What a pass is
One round of the notes. The order is: the rhythmic figure first, then the mode or chord, then the key — so one mode goes through every figure before the next mode starts.

Metronome

Tempo
10 to 300 BPM. Type it, drag it, or tap it in — Tap tempo averages the last few taps.
Bar
How many beats it counts. Tap a circle to cycle it: accented, plain, silent. With notes playing every beat is plain unless you ask otherwise; with the metronome alone the first beat is accented, so the bar you chose can be heard.
Subdivision
What each beat is divided into. Swing is two ticks split two to one — the triplet feel a written pair of eighths is actually played with. Single uses one figure; Sequence takes the ones you pick in turn, one per pass.
Click
Follow figure clicks every subdivision; Beats only clicks the beats and leaves the figure to the notes.
Advance Every
With the metronome alone nothing comes round on its own, so you say what a pass is: so many bars, or so many beats. The figure list and Speed Up both move on that.
Polyrhythm
Two pulses at once, any two numbers. The cycle shown is how long until they meet again. A bar and a subdivision mean nothing against two pulses, so they are put away while it is on.

Phrasing

Note Length
Per figure gives a note on every subdivision; Per beat one note a beat; Per bar one note a bar. Under swing the notes take the swing too, long then short.
Phrase Alignment
Let it drift is the exercise: a phrase whose length does not divide the bar walks round against the pulse and only comes back after several rounds, and the metronome is never resynchronised to it. Restart each round puts note one back on beat one every time.
Notes Follow
Under a polyrhythm, which of the two layers the notes ride.

Timbre

Why it matters
You tune by the beating between partials, so a voice with partials is worth more than a clean one. A sine has a single partial: excellent for a unison or an octave, useless for anything else.
Cello and Oboe
Recordings of real players, with the vibrato tracked out — a reference must not wobble — and retuned so every note lands within a cent.
Organ
Synthesised rather than recorded, from the harmonic profile of a church organ. Its pitch is exact by construction, at every note.
Real piano, Piano synth
For the note sequences, where a clear attack makes the rhythm of a line easy to hear. Both fade, so they are not offered as drones.
